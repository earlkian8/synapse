Everything that protects your account is under your account menu → **Settings** → **Security**. You are asked for your password before the page opens.

## Changing your password

Enter your current password, then the new one twice, and choose **Save**. Use a long password you don't use anywhere else — a password manager can make one for you.

## Two-factor authentication

With two-factor authentication on, signing in takes your password **and** a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password and similar). Someone who learns your password still can't get in.

**To turn it on:**

1. Choose **Enable 2FA**.
2. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or type in the setup key shown under it.
3. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows, and choose **Confirm**.

**Keep your recovery codes.** Once two-factor is on, choose **View recovery codes**. Store them somewhere safe and separate from your phone: each one signs you in once if you lose your phone. **Regenerate codes** replaces them all, and the old ones stop working.

**To turn it off**, choose **Disable 2FA**.

## Passkeys

A passkey lets you sign in with your device's own lock — fingerprint, face or PIN — instead of a password. It can't be phished or reused, because it never leaves your device.

- **Add one:** in **Passkeys**, choose **Add passkey**, give it a name you will recognise (such as "Work laptop"), and follow your browser's prompt.
- **Use it:** on the sign-in screen, choose **Sign in with a passkey**.
- **Remove one** you no longer use — for example from a device you have given away — from the same list. Each passkey shows when it was added and last used.

## If you think someone else has your password

1. Change your password straight away.
2. Turn on two-factor authentication.
3. Tell your HR or IT administrator, who can check the [activity logs](/help/administration/activity-logs) for anything done under your name.
