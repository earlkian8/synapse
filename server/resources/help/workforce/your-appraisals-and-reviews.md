Your appraisals, and the reviews you're asked to write, live under **Workforce** → **Performance Management**. The sections at the top of the page are **My appraisals**, **My goals** and **Reviews**. The number beside **Reviews** is how many reviews are waiting for you.

## My appraisals

**My appraisals** lists your appraisals, one per review cycle, newest first.

- **In progress**: your evaluator is still rating it. Its result isn't shown yet.
- **Being calibrated**: it has been submitted, but the people running the cycle are still comparing ratings across the company. You're told when it's ready to read.
- **Read and acknowledge**: it's ready. Open it to read it.
- **Acknowledged**: you, or HR on your behalf, have signed it off.

You're notified when an appraisal is ready to read.

### Reading an appraisal

An appraisal shows the result in your company's own words (for example _Meets Expectations_), the attainment behind it, and the **scorecard**. The scorecard lists each criterion with its rating and your evaluator's evidence, followed by their overall remarks. If you wrote a self-review, your own rating sits beside each of theirs under **You said**.

If your rating was moved in calibration, the appraisal says so. It shows what the scorecard gave and the rating now.

### Acknowledging it

Under **Acknowledge your appraisal**, you can add a comment and then choose **Acknowledge**. Your evaluator is told, and the appraisal becomes final.

Acknowledging says you've read it, not that you agree with it. Use the comment for anything you'd like on the record. Your comment stays with the appraisal and can't be changed later.

> [!NOTE]
> If you signed a paper copy instead, HR can record the sign-off for you. The appraisal then shows that HR recorded it.

## Reviews

**Reviews** lists what you've been asked to write, with the due date. The **To write** view shows the ones still waiting. Two kinds of review can come to you:

- **Your self-review**: your own view of your appraisal.
- **A review of a colleague**: what you see of someone you work with, report to or manage.

Open one and choose **Write it**.

### Writing a review

Rate each criterion on the same scale your evaluator uses. You can add a note to any rating. Then answer **What went well** and **What you'd like to develop**.

- **Save** keeps your draft. You can come back to it while the appraisal is in progress.
- **Hand in** sends it.
    - A self-review needs every criterion rated.
    - A colleague's review needs at least one rating or one written answer.
- **Decline** says you can't review this colleague, for example because you haven't worked together. The evaluator is told, with your reason if you give one.

You never see the evaluator's ratings while you write. Your review informs the appraisal but doesn't set its result: the evaluator reads it beside their own ratings.

### Who sees what you wrote

| Relationship                  | What the evaluator sees                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Self-review**               | Your answers, under your name.                                                                                             |
| **Manager**                   | Your answers, as the manager's view.                                                                                       |
| **Peer** or **direct report** | An average of everyone in that group, and their comments without names. Shown only once at least two people have answered. |

The person you review never sees your answers.

> [!TIP]
> If the appraisal is submitted before you hand in your review, the review closes and drops off your list. Nothing you saved is shared.
