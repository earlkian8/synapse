**Workforce** → **Attendance** is your team's daily time record. Clock-ins become worked hours, lateness, undertime and overtime, each person judged against the shift that applies to them that day and your company's [attendance policy](/help/company-setup/attendance-policies) — on your company's own clock, whatever time zone you are reading from.

## Finding your way around

The tiles at the top count who is present, late, absent and on leave, and the average hours worked. Under them:

- the **date stepper** — previous, **Today** (your company's today), next, or pick a date — moves by day, week or month depending on the tab;
- **search** and a **department** filter;
- three tabs over the same people.

Everyone appears, whether or not they punched: someone with no punches is shown as **Absent**, **Holiday**, **Day off** or **On leave**, whichever applies.

## Today's Log

**Exceptions** sit at the top — one row per problem, each with **Resolve** (or **View**):

- a missing time-out, or someone badly late;
- an unscheduled absence, or a day judged a half day;
- a punch made away from the site;
- a day closed automatically, or a punch from a phone whose clock was off.

When there are none, it says **All clear**.

Below it, the **daily log** lists each person's time in and out, hours, status and anything unusual (late by how much, a missing time-out, leaving early, a break that ran over). On today's date the status filter offers **Not clocked in yet**: people whose shift has started and who haven't clocked in, leaving out anyone on leave or a holiday.

## Weekly View

Employees down the side, Monday to Sunday across, each day a status tile — a holiday tile names the holiday. Click a tile, or a day's heading, to open that day's log.

## Monthly Report

One row per employee for the month: days present, times late, half days, absences, holidays, overtime (and how much of it awaits approval), night work where a policy counts it, and an attendance rate, with a small chart of hours worked. A holiday is neither attendance nor absence, so it doesn't count against the rate; a half day counts as attended.

**Payroll summary** downloads the month as a spreadsheet for payroll: one row per employee with days worked, absences, half days, late days and holidays, and worked, late, undertime, regular, overtime, approved overtime and night **minutes**. It contains no money — your payroll system turns minutes into pay.

## A day in detail

Click any person's day to open it. From the top:

- who and when, and **what the day was judged by**: the schedule, any holiday, and the attendance policy;
- the **totals** — worked, regular, break, late and overtime, plus night, rest-day and holiday minutes when there are any;
- the day's **flags**, such as _Half day_ or _Overtime awaiting approval_;
- the **punch trail**: each punch's time, how it was made, where (on site or how far away), whether it was sent offline, and its photo if one was taken. Punches replaced by a correction stay visible, struck through;
- remarks, and the sign-off.

## Statuses

| Status         | Meaning                                                                     |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Present**    | Worked the shift as expected.                                               |
| **Late**       | Clocked in after the shift's start plus any grace.                          |
| **Undertime**  | Left early, or worked short.                                                |
| **Half day**   | Late enough, or short enough, that the policy calls it a half day.          |
| **Absent**     | Due at work and didn't punch — or a policy threshold judged the day absent. |
| **On leave**   | Approved leave covers the day.                                              |
| **Holiday**    | A non-working holiday.                                                      |
| **Day off**    | Not a working day for this person.                                          |
| **Incomplete** | Clocked in, not yet out.                                                    |

## Days close themselves

Every hour, SYNAPSE closes the days that have ended on your company's clock: it records an absence for anyone who was due and never punched, deals with forgotten clock-outs the way your policy says, and then sends one summary of the day's exceptions to the people who review attendance.

> [!NOTE]
> A day is judged by the schedule and policy that applied when it opened. Changing a schedule or a policy later doesn't rewrite days already recorded — re-apply the rules if a past day should follow the change. See [Correcting and signing off attendance](/help/workforce/correcting-and-signing-off-attendance).
