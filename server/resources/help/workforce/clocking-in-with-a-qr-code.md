If your workplace has a QR station — a screen at the entrance showing a code — you clock in by scanning it with your phone. The screen says how: with the **SYNAPSE mobile app**, or with your phone's own camera.

## With the app

1. Open the app and go to **Clock**.
2. Tap **Scan to Time In** (or **Scan a QR station instead**, if you can also clock in the usual way).
3. Point your camera at the code on the screen. Hold still until it reads.
4. If your company asks for a selfie, take one.

The punch is recorded the moment the scan is accepted, and your name appears on the screen.

## With your phone's camera

Where the screen says to use your phone's camera:

1. Open the camera and point it at the code. Tap the link that appears.
2. Type your **employee number** (the digits are enough) and your **PIN**, the one you use at kiosks. HR issues it if you don't have one.
3. Allow the page to use your location when your phone asks.
4. The first time, choose a PIN of your own.
5. Tap **Time in** (or whatever you're punching). Take a photo first if your company asks for one.

The page shows the time it recorded. You have three minutes from scanning to finish; after that, scan the code again.

## Why a scan is turned away

The app says why, and what to do:

- **The code has already changed.** The code changes every few seconds. Scan the one showing now.
- **You're away from the station.** You need to be at the site where the screen is.
- **Your location is too rough.** Turn on precise location for SYNAPSE, step near a window, and scan again.
- **Your phone says its location is simulated.** Turn off any app that changes your location.
- **This phone clocked in somebody else today.** Everyone scans with their own phone.
- **Connect to the office Wi-Fi.** Some stations only take scans on the office network. Turn off mobile data or join the Wi-Fi, then scan again.
- **That number and PIN don't match.** Check both. Five wrong tries lock your PIN for 15 minutes; HR can issue a new one.

A QR punch needs a connection: it can't be saved on the phone and sent later the way an ordinary punch can.
