An **attendance policy** decides how a working day is judged: how much lateness is forgiven, when a day becomes a half day, what counts as overtime and whether it needs approval, how breaks and night work are treated, and how punches may be made. You start from a preset and change the rules, each written as a plain sentence — there are no formulas to write.

Find them under **Company Setup** → **Attendance Policies**.

## Making a policy

Choose **New policy**. The editor has a list of topics on the left (along the top on a phone). Work through them with **Next**, or jump to any one; each topic in the list says in a line what it's set to, so you can read a whole policy without opening anything.

1. **About this policy.** Pick where the rules start, give the policy a name, and say who it's for. Tick **Make this the company default** for the policy most people follow.
2. Go through the topics and change what your company does differently.
3. Choose **Create policy**.

Every rule is written as a sentence with the numbers filled in, such as *Count it as a half day when someone is more than [2 hours] late.* Tick a rule to turn it on; a rule that's off stays readable, greyed out. Typing in a greyed-out rule's box turns it on. Lengths of time can be in minutes or hours: pick the unit beside the number.

### Where the rules start

| Start from                   | In short                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Philippines — Labor Code** | Overtime after 8 hours a day, needing approval; a 1-hour unpaid lunch taken off after 5 hours if none was punched; night differential 22:00–06:00. |
| **Standard 40-hour week**    | Overtime after 40 hours a week; a 30-minute unpaid break after 6 hours.                                                                            |
| **Flexible, no lateness**    | Nobody is judged late; a day is short only on hours.                                                                                               |
| **Shift work**               | Times rounded to the nearest 15 minutes; overtime after 8 hours a day and 40 a week; a forgotten clock-out closed 2 hours after the shift.         |
| **A plain start**            | Exact times, each schedule's own grace, overtime after the shift's hours, breaks as punched.                                                       |

Picking another start replaces the rules with its own. A policy made from a preset can be put back to it at any time with **Put its rules back**, under **About this policy**.

## The topics

- **Late arrivals.** Whether people are marked late at all; how much lateness you let go (the first few minutes each day, each schedule's own grace, or an allowance for the month); when lateness makes it a **half day**, or **absent**.
- **Short days.** Whether a day is short when someone leaves before the shift ends, or only when they work too few hours; when a short day becomes a half day or absent.
- **Breaks.** An unpaid break taken off when nobody clocks out for one; how much of a punched break is paid; the longest a break may run before the extra counts as leaving early.
- **Overtime & night work.** Daily, weekly, both or none; when a day's overtime starts (after the shift's hours, or after a set number); when a week's starts; the smallest amount that counts; whether overtime must be **approved** first; whether early clock-ins, rest days and holidays count; and the night hours set apart for the night differential.
- **Clocking in.** The ways people may clock in (the mobile app, a [QR station](/help/workforce/qr-stations), a [kiosk](/help/workforce/attendance-kiosks), the web, or entered by HR); whether a **photo** goes with every app, QR and kiosk punch; where they may clock in from (anywhere, anywhere but flagged away from your [sites](/help/company-setup/work-locations), or only at your sites); which networks web punches must come from; reminders for people who haven't clocked in; and what happens when someone forgets to clock out.
- **Fine-tuning.** Rarely changed: rounding clock times to 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes (the punches themselves never change); how early a clock-in counts toward a shift; the longest a shift can run; how old an offline punch from the app may be; and how far off a phone's clock may be before it's flagged.

## The sample day

Beside the rules (under them on a phone, as **Try these rules on a sample day**), a **sample day** shows what your rules make of a day: choose a working day, rest day or holiday, the shift, the clock-in and clock-out, and a break. It shows the status and flags, regular and overtime minutes (and whether overtime awaits approval), lateness and how much was let go, short time, the break, and night, rest-day and holiday minutes. It updates a moment after you stop typing, using exactly the rules attendance will use.

## Which policy applies to a day

The most specific one wins:

1. the policy named on a schedule **assignment** for that date;
2. the policy on the **schedule** that day's shift comes from;
3. the person's **department's** policy;
4. their main **work location's** policy;
5. the **company default** (the star);
6. the built-in rules — exact punch times, each schedule's own grace, overtime as anything worked beyond the day's hours (never needing approval), breaks exactly as punched, and no thresholds or night differential.

> [!NOTE]
> A company that configures nothing is judged by the built-in rules.

## Changing a policy

Each day keeps the policy it was judged by when it opened. Editing or archiving a policy changes how **new** days are judged, never days already recorded. To bring past days in line, [re-apply the rules](/help/workforce/correcting-and-signing-off-attendance).

A policy still named by a schedule, department, location or assignment can't be deleted permanently — archive it instead.
