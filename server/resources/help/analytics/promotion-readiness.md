**Analytics & AI** → **Promotion Readiness** compares each active employee's appraisal record with the records of people who were promoted — to support fair, evidence-based decisions about advancement and succession.

> [!NOTE]
> Readiness describes a record, not a person's worth. Use it to make sure nobody ready is overlooked — the decision stays yours.

## Running an assessment

Choose **Run assessment**. Everyone with at least one **completed** appraisal is assessed, and the result is kept as a snapshot in the history selector.

Nobody is scored from a guess. People the model can't assess — no completed appraisal yet, or only one still in draft — are listed under **Not assessed**, with the reason and what would include them.

## What it looks at

Only two things: the result of the person's **latest completed appraisal**, and **how much it changed** from the one before (when there is one before). Department, salary, length of service, employment type and anything about who the person is are never used.

Someone with two appraisals is assessed on both; someone with one is assessed on that one alone, and the page says which.

## Reading the page

The tiles count who was assessed, how many are promotion-ready and developing, and the average readiness. The **ranked roster** lists everyone by readiness score with their tier, whether the score rests on one appraisal or two, and their strongest positive factor. Search, or filter by tier.

The score (0–100) places the person among a reference workforce. The tier — **High**, **Medium** or **Low** — compares their chance of promotion with the average:

| Tier       | Chance of promotion compared with the average |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **High**   | At least twice the average                    |
| **Medium** | At least the average                          |
| **Low**    | Below the average                             |

Click someone to read it in plain words, for example "12% of people with this record were promoted within a year — 1.2× the average", along with what each appraisal fact added or took away and the appraisals it was built from.

## How much to trust it

Until your company has enough history of its own, the model compares your people with a large reference workforce. Your company's own model learns who actually gets promoted here — see [Training a model on your own records](/help/analytics/model-graduation).
