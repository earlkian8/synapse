**Analytics & AI** → **Performance Forecast** projects each active employee's **next appraisal result**, with the range it is likely to land in — to plan reviews, coaching and development before the cycle, not after it.

> [!NOTE]
> A forecast is a planning aid, not a rating. Each appraisal is still decided on the scorecard.

## Running a forecast

Choose **Run forecast**. SYNAPSE forecasts the next review cycle that isn't closed yet, from each person's latest **completed** appraisal before that cycle — never from the cycle it is forecasting. Past forecasts stay in the history selector; delete one you no longer need.

People who can't be forecast — no completed appraisal yet, one still in draft, or appraised only in the cycle being forecast — are listed under **Not forecast**, each with the reason.

## Reading the page

The tiles count who was forecast, how many are expected to **exceed**, the average forecast and the average confidence. The **ranked roster** lists everyone by forecast result with their band, likely range, and how it compares with their last appraisal. Search, or filter by band.

| Band         | Forecast result |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| **Below**    | Under 60        |
| **On track** | 60 to under 80  |
| **Exceeds**  | 80 and over     |

Click someone to see:

- the **forecast result** and the **range** that four in five next results land in;
- the **band**, and the **chance it is right** — near a band's edge, honestly, that's close to a coin flip;
- a **trajectory chart** of their completed appraisals leading to the forecast — and, once their next appraisal is done, the actual result beside it;
- the appraisal the forecast rests on.

## How it did

Once the forecast cycle has completed appraisals, **How this forecast did** compares the forecast with what happened: the average miss, how many results landed inside their range (it promises about four in five), and how often the band was right. It waits for at least 20 completed appraisals before giving a verdict.

## How much to trust it

Until your company has enough appraisal history of its own, the model has learned from a large reference workforce. Your company's own model, once it can be trained, learns how ratings move here — see [Training a model on your own records](/help/analytics/model-graduation).
