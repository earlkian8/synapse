Open a posting from **Recruitment** to see its **pipeline**: everyone who applied, and how far each of them has got.

## The pipeline page

The header shows the posting, its status, its closing-date countdown and its screening criteria. Below it, **decision support** shows the counts for the stage you are focused on and one line on what to do next.

There are two views, switched in the toolbar (your choice is remembered on this browser):

- **Table** — the default. Each candidate's fit, stage, rating, interviews and days since applying, 25 at a time. Filter by stage, search, and sort — **Best match** keeps the ranking by fit.
- **Board** — one column per stage, in your pipeline's order. Each card has a one-click **Advance**.

Click a candidate to open their full profile.

## Adding candidates

Candidates arrive on their own when they apply on your [careers page](/help/talent-acquisition/careers-page). To add one yourself — someone referred, or a CV that came by email — choose **Add candidate** and enter their details, résumé and any supporting documents. Somebody already in your candidate pool can be added to another posting without retyping them.

## Moving candidates through stages

- **Advance** moves a candidate to the next stage of the pipeline.
- **Move to…** (in a candidate's **⋯** menu) puts them on any stage.
- **Reject** ends their application, with an optional reason. A rejected candidate can be moved back later.

The **Hired** stage can only be reached by hiring — see [Interviews and hiring](/help/talent-acquisition/interviews-and-hiring).

## The fit score

Unless the posting turned ranking off, every candidate gets a **fit score** from 0 to 100, with a band — **strong**, **promising**, **fair** or **weak**. It is worked out from what you know so far:

| Part                                                           | Weight |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Your rating of the candidate                                   | 30     |
| Years of experience against the posting's minimum              | 25     |
| The posting's skills shown in their résumé, profile and letter | 20     |
| How their interviews went                                      | 15     |
| How complete their documents are                               | 10     |

Parts that don't apply yet — no minimum set, no interview held — are left out, and the score is worked out from the rest. So ranking starts the moment someone applies, and sharpens as you rate, interview and set criteria. The candidate's profile shows the breakdown.

### How skills are matched

SYNAPSE reads the résumé itself (PDF, Word, or a scan or photo), not only the profile. A skill counts when the candidate:

- **names it**, in any of its forms: "JS", "NodeJS", "Postgres";
- shows a skill that **proves** it: TypeScript for JavaScript, Next.js for React;
- shows **all its parts**: React and Laravel for full-stack.

A **related** skill earns part of the credit: JavaScript when you asked for TypeScript, Vue when you asked for React. A must-have counts twice a nice-to-have, and a missing skill lowers the score but never hides anyone.

Whole words only: "Java" is not found in "JavaScript", and "excel at" is not Excel. Under the skill match, the profile lists each skill and how it was shown, for example "JavaScript — shown by TypeScript · résumé". It also says when a résumé couldn't be read.

If a candidate left their years of experience blank, the dates of the jobs on their résumé are added up instead. These years are shown as an estimate, for example "≈6 yrs from résumé".

## The candidate profile

Opening a candidate shows everything in one place: contact details and links, every document, the fit breakdown, a stage stepper built from your pipeline, interview history, and their applications to your other postings.

The **decision panel** recommends the next step — advance to a named stage, hire, reject, or nothing yet — with a button that does it. It is a recommendation: you decide.

## AI insights

**AI Insights** asks an AI model to read the candidate's actual résumé and documents against the role and your criteria. It returns a verdict, strengths, concerns, what the documents reveal, interview questions worth asking, and a recommendation.

The read is saved, so reopening the candidate shows it instantly; **Regenerate** asks again. Government ID documents are never sent to the model.

> [!NOTE]
> If the AI service is busy, you'll see a message asking you to try again shortly. Nothing else on the page is affected.
