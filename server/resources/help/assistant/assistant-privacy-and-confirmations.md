The assistant is powerful, so it works inside firm limits. This is what they are.

## It can only do what you can do

The assistant acts **as you**. It is offered only the actions your role allows, and every action is checked again when it runs, exactly as on the screens. It cannot reach another company's data, and it cannot grant anyone — including you — more access.

## It asks before anything that matters

Some actions run straight away when you ask, like adding a note or recording an interview outcome. Others wait for you — for example:

- anything that **notifies people**, such as inviting attendees or sending an announcement;
- anything **hard to undo**, such as completing an exit, deleting a record or archiving;
- anything affecting **many records at once**, such as signing off every pending attendance day or re-applying rules to a month.

For these, the assistant shows a card saying exactly what will happen — including what follows from it, such as how many people are affected — with **Confirm** and **Cancel**. Until you press **Confirm**, nothing has changed. Pressing **Cancel** leaves everything as it was.

## What it never shows in chat

Whatever your role, the assistant never reveals these in a conversation:

- pay and salary;
- government ID numbers;
- bank details;
- home addresses;
- dates of birth;
- passwords.

They stay where they belong — in the 201 file, behind the permissions that guard it. The assistant can still **record** them when you give them to it, for example when adding an employee. It never handles passwords at all: change yours in your [security settings](/help/your-account/password-and-security).

## Everything is recorded

Every change the assistant makes is written to the [activity logs](/help/administration/activity-logs) under your name, marked **via assistant**. Reading a named person's record is recorded too. So it is always possible to see who did what — and whether they did it on a screen or in the chat.

## It treats records as information, not instructions

Records can contain text written by anyone — an applicant's cover letter, a note on a task. The assistant reads such text as information about the record, never as instructions to follow. It never shows images, and it only links to pages inside SYNAPSE.

## Predictions are handled with care

When it talks about attrition risk, forecasts or promotion readiness, the assistant treats them as prompts for a supportive conversation or for planning — never as grounds for discipline, and never as something to tell the person concerned.
